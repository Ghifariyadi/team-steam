ADDITIONAL RULE FOR harm_mention: CHEATING ALONE IS NOT TOXICITY

This rule narrows the explicit-cheating item of the toxicity definition above.

A complaint about cheating, hackers, aimbots, wallhacks, smurfs, modders, boosting or other unfair play
by other players is NOT toxicity by itself. Answer "no" when cheating is the only problem the review
describes, even if the reviewer is angry, says the game is ruined, or says the community is "full of cheaters".

A review that complains about cheating is still "yes" only if it ALSO contains at least one of these:
- insults or slurs aimed at people: players, teammates, a nationality, an ethnicity, a gender or another group.
  Blaming cheating on a nationality or group is a stereotype and counts (for example "Chinese players
  and hackers ruined this game");
- threats;
- harassment or abusive voice or text chat;
- deliberate disruption or griefing of other players, apart from the cheating itself;
- an explicitly toxic community or atmosphere described apart from the cheating itself.

Insults or profanity aimed only at the developer or the company do not count by themselves.
A complaint about being banned unfairly, or about the game's anti-cheat, is not toxicity.

Examples of the pattern (paraphrased):
- "Too many cheaters, please fix it" -> no.
- "Almost every online lobby has modders with god mode" -> no.
- "Hackers everywhere and teammates screaming slurs in the mic" -> yes (the slurs and the abusive chat).
- "Every cheater is from one particular country" -> yes (nationality stereotype).
- "Hackers in every match, the devs are idiots" -> no (insult aimed only at the developer).

When you answer "yes", the reason must name the qualifying element that is NOT cheating.
