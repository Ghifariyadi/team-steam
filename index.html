We give Gemini each review in its original language, and only translate it so we can read it. Emotion is easier to catch before translation, because machine translation tends to lose or flatten it. Kabir et al., 2025

04 · ONE RULE ADDED LATER

One more rule.

After the first results we added one rule to the codebook: complaining about cheaters alone is not toxic. Gemini then re-checked every review it had called toxic.

The rule came after we saw results, so it is a choice we made, not a blind test.

Interpretation

How you define “toxic” changes the answer.

✓

The definition mattered more than the model.

One extra rule, that cheating alone is not toxic, cut the toxicity rate roughly in half. Same AI, same reviews.

✓

Every label comes with a reason.

So we could audit them one by one, and spotted what to fix that way.

✓

Counting is not explaining.

We can say how many reviews mention toxicity. We cannot say what causes it.

Limitations

Three games, for now.

We only cover three games: Counter-Strike 2, PUBG and GTA V. That also keeps the genres limited, since they are all action games, mostly shooters. So the numbers speak for these reviews, not for every kind of game on Steam.

Next, we can run the same codebook on more games and more genres, and see if the patterns hold.

Recommendation for Steam

What Steam could do next.

We know game communities tend to be toxic. But racism and insulting someone’s identity cannot be justified. Here are four patterns from our data that Steam could turn into guidelines, with the reason and the indicators behind each label.

PATTERN 01 · 48.5% OF TOXIC REVIEWS

Insults aimed at other players

Suggested guideline

Criticize the play, not the person. Name-calling aimed at a player should be removable.

Why we labeled it toxic
  • Words that attack a person’s skill, intelligence or character, like idiot, trash or worse.
  • The target is a person. In 71% of these reviews Gemini marked players in general, and in 10% a teammate.
“First up are the trolls. A bunch of no-life cunts whom nobody likes…”Polish, translated
PATTERN 02 · 25.9% OF TOXIC REVIEWS

Attacks on someone’s identity

Suggested guideline

No attacks on who someone is: nationality, ethnicity, race, gender, sexuality, religion, age or disability. That includes blaming a whole nationality for cheating.

Why we labeled it toxic
  • A group word together with a slur, an insult or a blame. Complaining about cheating alone does not count, but blaming it on a nationality does.
  • In 88% of these reviews the group is a nationality or ethnicity, and 81% are aimed at a nationality group.
“This game is so much fun when those motherfucking Russians aren’t around.”Turkish, translated
PATTERN 03 · 20.1% OF TOXIC REVIEWS

Swearing

Suggested guideline

Swearing alone should stay allowed, especially at the game or the developer. It becomes a problem when it is aimed at a person.

Why we labeled it toxic
  • Obscene language that is not clearly aimed at a person or a group.
  • This is the weakest pattern. 41% of these reviews swear at the developer, which our own rule says is not toxic on its own. 48% are aimed at players in general.
“*I shoot 10 year old teammate accidently* Me: sorry Kid: FUCKING TEAMKILLER”English
PATTERN 04 · 6.2% OF TOXIC REVIEWS

Threats and wishing harm

Suggested guideline

Threats and wishing someone dead or sick should be removable.

Why we labeled it toxic
  • Words that say or wish harm on someone, like dying, cancer or a painful disease.
  • The rarest of the four, and 60% of these reviews are aimed at players in general.
“…smurfs who come back in full force every time there’s a sale (may you die a slow and painful disease, guys)…”French, translated
✓What to leave alone: criticizing the game, bug and price complaints, and reports of cheaters on their own. Most “Not recommended” reviews (71.6%) were not flagged as toxic.

Shares are of the 1,669 toxic reviews, and a review can have several types. Gemini used eight types. The other four are smaller or are not a behavior a guideline can target: reviews that describe a toxic community (11.1%), other (7.8%), sexual harassment (1.0%) and griefing or trolling (0.8%). These are suggestions from our data. We did not test whether guidelines like these reduce toxicity, and the labels are not checked against humans yet.
