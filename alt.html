Is half of Steam toxic?

02Question

What do players write when a game goes wrong?

Reviews are a public record of how multiplayer games feel to play. We looked for three things.

ToxicityInsults, slurs and threats, as the review mentions them.
IronyA “10/10” that is really a complaint.
Recommend?Do toxic reviews recommend the game less?

Research question: How do literal, mixed and ironic evaluations intersect with gender stereotypes and accounts of harassment, and do reviews that describe toxic communities recommend games less?

03Data

Three games. Mostly not in English.

7,365Steam reviews
3Counter-Strike 2, PUBG, GTA V
80%are not in English

Source: KieranPoc’s Kaggle Steam reviews dataset, collected through the Steam reviews API. We read each review in its original language.

04Method

One AI. One rulebook. Two humans to check.

  1. i.

    Write the rulebook

    A codebook says exactly what counts as toxic and what counts as ironic.

  2. ii.

    Gemini reads every review

    One at a time, in the original language. It answers yes, no or uncertain, and gives a reason.

  3. iii.

    Humans check

    Two annotators labeled 90 reviews blind. They agreed almost every time (kappa 0.85 and 0.90).

05The twist
22.7%
1,669 still toxicsomething beyond cheating
1,829 flippedmostly cheating complaints

First pass: Gemini called 3,499 reviews toxic. Almost half.

Then we read them. Most were people yelling about cheaters.

So we added one rule: cheating alone is not toxic.

With the rule, 22.7% of the reviews mention toxicity.

First pass: 47.5 percent of 7,363 reviews flagged toxic. After the rule that cheating alone is not toxic: 22.7 percent. 1,829 reviews flipped to not toxic.

06Finding 1 of 3
Where

Counter-Strike 2 is the most toxic, and the most ironic.

Mentions toxicity, with the cheating ruleIronic

Overall: 22.7% mention toxicity and 25.0% are ironic, out of 7,363 labeled reviews. Before the rule, toxicity was 49.9% in Counter-Strike 2, 39.7% in GTA V and 50.9% in PUBG.

Share of the 1,669 toxic reviews with each type; a review can have several. Examples are real review text, picked as the clearest. Types are Gemini’s labels, and some phrases are jokes or sarcasm.

08Finding 3 of 3
Does it matter

Players who don’t recommend a game are toxic more often.

28.4%of “Not recommended” reviews2,369 reviews
19.9%of “Recommended” reviews4,994 reviews

This is a link, not a cause. Someone who dislikes a game may also be more likely to complain about other players.

09Interpretation

How you define “toxic” changes the answer.

1

The definition mattered more than the model.

One extra rule moved the toxicity rate from 47.5% to 22.7%. Same AI, same reviews.

2

Every label comes with a reason.

So we can audit the labels one by one, and we found the cheating problem that way.

3

Counting is not explaining.

We can say how many reviews mention toxicity. We cannot say what causes it.

10Limitations

What this does not show.

1

Humans have not checked these labels yet.

The two annotators agreed with each other on 90 reviews. We have not compared Gemini with humans on the full 7,363.

2

About 80% of the reviews are not in English.

We tuned and tested the rulebook mostly on English. Gemini outside English is untested.

3

We wrote the cheating rule after seeing the first result.

So 22.7% is not a blind result. And it is three games, not all of Steam.

11Check our work

Before you trust a toxicity number, ask how toxic was defined.
